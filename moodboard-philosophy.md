# Taxonomy of Presence
### A design philosophy for the actress portfolio mood board

---

**The work at hand is not a single portfolio. It is a catalog of *presences*** — ten discrete atmospheres in which a performer can be met for the first time. Each vibe is a room; each room has its own weather, its own light, its own silence. The catalog itself is an artifact: a reference object built to be held, flipped, and returned to. It is edited with the restraint of a gallery curator and the devotion of a rare-book designer.

**Space is the loudest voice.** Margins are not leftover area; they are structural. White (and its cousins: cream, bone, ivory, ash) is given as much weight as any image. Each spread breathes. Each element is placed — never dropped, never floated. The negative space is the stage; the performer arrives only when the stage is prepared. Every alignment is deliberate, every proportion weighed, the result of countless small adjustments by a hand that refuses the approximate.

**Typography is cast, not chosen.** Each vibe is rendered in typefaces that would have auditioned for the role: a Didone for the playbill, a vertical CJK serif for Hong Kong rain, a copperplate script for Shanghai lacquer. Words appear only as labels — a name, a palette code, a single suitability note — never as explanation. The typography is the atmosphere; the reader feels the era, the genre, the mood before they have read a single word. Hierarchy is built through scale and silence, not bold tags and underlines. Where a bilingual voice earns its place — Mandarin, Hangul, Japanese, Devanagari — the second script arrives as a quiet equal, not a decoration.

**Color operates as temperature, not decoration.** Each vibe is limited to a palette of four to six hues, quoted in their precise notation so a collaborator could reproduce them without guessing. The palettes are restrained — no hue appears for novelty. A single lacquer red against a field of bone carries more weight than a rainbow. The catalog's through-line is restraint: within each vibe, the restraint becomes a different kind of music.

**The performer's likeness is abstracted, never faked.** Where a headshot would live, a quiet frame — a tonal rectangle, a shaped silhouette, a cropped gesture — holds the space with dignity. The mood board does not impersonate the actress; it prepares the room for her. This is a rehearsal, not a performance. The placeholder is itself a design decision, composed with the same care as a final image.

**Everything must read as meticulously crafted — the product of master-level execution.** Every spread should feel painstakingly considered: the kerning, the rule-weights, the paper-colored grounds, the sequence. The catalog should feel like it was labored over by someone at the top of their field, someone who understands that an actress's first impression is a work of atmosphere, not information. The final object earns the word *pristine*. It is as much a gift to the performer as it is a tool.

---

### The ten vibes (in sequence)

1. **Cinéma Noir** — A24 prestige indie, late-night cinema poster glow
2. **Hong Kong Neon** — Wong Kar-wai, *In the Mood for Love*, *2046*, rain-blurred humid night
3. **Shanghai Deco** — 1930s chinoiserie, Zhang Yimou cinnabar, gold-leaf ornament
4. **Bollywood Maximal** — jewel saturation, marigold, gold filigree, ornament as structure
5. **Korean Hanok Prestige** — Park Chan-wook austerity, bone, ink, aged wood, red seal
6. **Japanese Photobook** — Araki and Ozu, wabi-sabi, generous silence, vertical kanji, hanko
7. **French Nouvelle Vague** — Godard and Varda, newsprint grid, red-black-cream, jump-cut
8. **Wes Anderson Symmetry** — pastel precision, centered composition, typewriter serif
9. **Vogue Editorial** — fashion-forward, magazine-grade, commercial high-gloss
10. **Commercial Fresh** — lifestyle, campaign, warm brand work, DTC cheer

### Book-object structure (15 spreads)

Cover · Contents · Foreword · Ten Vibes · Suitability Matrix · Colophon.
A foreword opens the book as a manifesto. A suitability matrix closes the catalog with a practical cross-reference. A colophon — paper, typefaces, palette notation — closes the book as a printed object would. The structure itself performs the same restraint the pages ask of their designer.

Each vibe is a door. Only one will feel like home.
